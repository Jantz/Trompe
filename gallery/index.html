Trompe · Material studies

Interfaces you want to touch

Eight everyday app screens, each built from a different material world, drawn entirely in CSS and SVG with no images. Buttons, inputs, switches, menus, tabs and dialogs keep their native behaviour and keyboard support; they just happen to be made of lacquer, oak, silicone or glass. One scene light re-lights every surface. Open one and turn the light dial in its top bar.

One light

Every shadow, rim, gloss and socket reads --tr-light-angle. Turn it and the whole object agrees on where the light is, embossing and needle shadows included.

Real controls

Standard controls stay standard: native buttons, inputs and dialogs, menus with full keyboard support. Where a control needs physics, shared controllers add press travel, detents, over-centre throws and interlocking keys, and still fire native events.

Felt, not just seen

Clicks, thunks and paper rustles are synthesized per material from filtered noise and short resonances, with haptics on phones. The speaker button in each bar turns them off.

Fast by construction

Geometry is in em off container queries, so objects scale like vector drawings and re-flow on phones. Motion is transform and opacity only, textures are tiled once, and nothing animates at rest.

Every product here is invented. Build your own from the authoring guide.
